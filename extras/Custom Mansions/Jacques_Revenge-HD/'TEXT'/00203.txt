Jacques: "... Then the Cardinal would be convicted by both secular and church law!"Comte: "He would lose everything overnight. The King already doesn't like His Eminence as it is. If he was informed of all his misdeeds he would have him stripped of all power, wealth and dignity and we might get a very fat, very ruined, very brassed off cellmate very soon!"The Comte had died just two days before the Bastille fell. When the people opened the gates of the prison Jacques went to the Palais Cremebrule, the place where all the events were set in motion. The Cardinal had bought the palace after the Comte had been sent to prison and ordered to connect his new residence with the adjacent archbishop's palace, now his seat of office, to make it the biggest aristocratic house in Paris. Work only just begun and it would never be finished. Because as soon as the people of Paris sensed the air of revolt and anarchy, they remembered their biggest enemy and suppressor. They stormed the two splendid palaces and within hours turned a kingdom's worth of luxury, beauty and art into heaps of smoldering rubble.

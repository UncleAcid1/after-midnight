Close to the balcony Jacques saw a large group of peasants that dragged the Cardinal and thirteen other bishops along with them. Within less than an hour these men had been turned from haughty, opulent, beaming millionaires to humiliated, powerless beggars. Among the bishops Jacques recognized the archbishop of Toulouse. He was standing fully dressed up to his chest in a huge barrel of molasses, which was pulled along on a cart by a band of laughing peasants. Some other bishops were covered almost from head to toe in the remains of what must have been dozens of enormous cream-pies. Another one looked like he had been subjected to a thorough mud-bath somewhere on the grounds. The priceless robes of all the other bishops had been torn and cut to the tiniest of shreds. Peasants held up the ends of their silken trains, thereby adding to the humiliation, because the trains were nothing more than besmeared rags.While marvelling at the destructive creativity of the peasants, Jacques did also feel a genuine compassion for the prelates.

From a little balcony that survived the explosion Jacques took a look around and saw the extent of the destruction. The main palace was torn apart in different places by multiple powder explosions. Only the center part with the grand vestibule and the enormous marble feast-hall above it seemed untouched. The rest of the building was for the larger part either in flames or already in ruins. From those parts of the buildings that were still intact, furniture and objects of art were flying out the windows and going to pieces as soon as they hit the ground. The peasants had turned their attention towards the remaining smaller palaces, the pavilions, the galleries of statues and even the church. Huge and splendid gilt carriages were dragged across the grounds, run into walls and set aflame. Apparently the peasants also already had plundered some of the dressing rooms of the Cardinal. A handful of men had put on the magnificent robes, looking ridiculous wearing silks and furs tailored to fit the Cardinal. They started a mock procession which ended in a field of mud where construction of a large pool was in process. Frolicking around like children, they threw the mud at each other and after completely ruining the robes they just threw them off and trampled them underfoot.

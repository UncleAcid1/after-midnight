"Man! Still more gold! This is it! I made it! The loot of a lifetime! Oh, I am so gonna buy that patent of nobility and a chateau on the Loire! And then the career will really take off! I have enough money now to start my own army of robbers! If I walk among the nobles as a peer, how hard can it be to find out where they keep their treasures, how well they are guarded and when they are on vacation? And then me and my men just have to break into the empty palaces and chateaus, strip them down to the naked walls and go back home! Those powdered sissies will never know what hit them! While the peasants and the poor will be pleasantly surprised to find some gold on the doorsteps of their huts every now and then! Yeah! From now on it's peace to the huts and war to the palaces!* Oh... That's good! I gotta write that down!"(* A few weeks later, the autor Nicolas Chamfort finds a snippet of text on a lonely alley close to the Bastille. It only contains one sentence: "Peace to the huts, war to the palaces!" The misantropic and discontented bastard of a cathedral canon and an aristocratic lady publishes this aphorism and it will soon encourage peasants all over the country to thoroughly follow its suggestion.)
